# India Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Policy Rate Tightening & Systemic Liquidity Absorption
- **Condition:** Headline retail CPI breaches the RBI's 6.0% statutory upper tolerance band on the rebased (2024=100) series, prompting money markets (OIS, T-bills) to price in a 25 to 50 bps repo rate hike cycle from the 5.25% baseline alongside aggressive liquidity absorption via Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) operations.
- **Action:** Bullish on 1-year and 2-year Overnight Index Swap (OIS) paying positions, 91-day sovereign T-bills, and ultra-short duration liquidity funds; Bearish on 10-year benchmark G-Secs, long-duration debt mutual funds, and wholesale credit-dependent Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs).

## Rule 2: Agricultural Supply Shocks & Core "Shrinkflation" Propagation
- **Condition:** Volatile food and agricultural commodity prices propel headline CPI persistently above 6.0% for consecutive quarters, driving consumer goods manufacturers to implement pervasive "shrinkflation" and structural price pass-through into core baskets despite restrictive monetary policy.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic agrochemical producers, fertilizer distributors, gold-loan NBFCs, and large-cap FMCG leaders with inelastic rural distribution networks; Bearish on mass-market volume-sensitive consumer durables, entry-level two-wheeler OEMs, and sub-scale quick-service restaurant (QSR) chains.

## Rule 3: Credit-Deposit Ratio Divergence & Commercial Bank NIM Compression
- **Condition:** Commercial bank credit growth outstrips domestic deposit accretion amidst tighter liquidity, compelling lenders to aggressively hike retail term deposit rates as funding costs accelerate faster than external benchmark-linked lending rate (EBLR) repricing.
- **Action:** Bullish on low Credit-to-Deposit Ratio (CDR) public sector banks with dominant low-cost CASA franchises, institutional wealth managers, and short-tenor commercial paper; Bearish on high-CDR private commercial banks, standalone housing finance companies (HFCs), and vehicle finance NBFCs.

## Rule 4: Sovereign Yield Curve Bear Flattening & Supply Indigestion
- **Condition:** Heavy central fiscal borrowing targets converge with front-loaded State Development Loan (SDL) supply while front-end yields spike under anticipated outsized MPC policy tightening and reduced commercial bank Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) buying appetite.
- **Action:** Bullish on sovereign floating-rate bonds (FRBs), primary dealer short-duration market-making books, and low-duration liquid cash instruments; Bearish on 10-year, 30-year, and 40-year nominal sovereign G-Secs, long-dated corporate infrastructure paper, and duration bond ETFs.

## Rule 5: Rupee Depreciation Pressures & External Deficit Defense
- **Condition:** Merchandise trade deficits widen and external capital volatility weakens the Indian Rupee toward fresh record lows against the US Dollar, prompting aggressive multi-market RBI interventions across spot, onshore forwards, and offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) liquidity pools.
- **Action:** Bullish on Tier-1 IT services exporters, contract Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API) manufacturers, and USD/INR long carry positions via offshore forward points; Bearish on domestic commercial airlines, corporate issuers with unhedged External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs), and import-dependent consumer electronics assemblers.

## Rule 6: FPI Flow Volatility vs. Resilient Domestic SIP Liquidity
- **Condition:** Global macro risk-off rotations and elevated domestic equity valuations drive sharp Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI) capital outflows during holiday-shortened trading windows, countered by uninterrupted domestic retail Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) mutual fund inflows.
- **Action:** Bullish on defensive Nifty 50 mega-cap bellwethers, domestic equity exchange operators, central depository service providers (CDSL), and domestic asset management companies (AMCs); Bearish on high-beta small-and-mid-cap (SMID) equities trading at extreme forward P/E multiples and cash-burning consumer internet platforms.

## Rule 7: Imported Hydrocarbon Shocks & Regulated Downstream Margin Squeeze
- **Condition:** International Brent crude and imported LNG prices surge alongside escalating geopolitical tensions, while state directives restrain or freeze retail transport fuel pump prices to mitigate headline inflation contagion.
- **Action:** Bullish on upstream domestic oil and gas exploration and production (E&P) firms, domestic coal producers, and renewable independent power producers (IPPs); Bearish on state-owned downstream Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs), synthetic chemical manufacturers, and industrial paint producers.

## Rule 8: Global Supply Chain Re-Shoring & PLI Greenfield Capex
- **Condition:** Multinationals accelerate "China+1" supply chain diversification into India, supported by expanding operational disbursements under Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes and fortified international trade integration.
- **Action:** Bullish on Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) contractors, precision engineering exporters, industrial automation providers, and private commercial port operators; Bearish on commoditized low-tech textile fabricators and secondary steel producers vulnerable to regional product dumping.

## Rule 9: Sovereign Capital Outlay Expansion & Infrastructure Fixed Capital Formation
- **Condition:** Central and state capital expenditure programs expand at double-digit rates, targeting strategic transportation corridors, high-speed rail, national power grid enhancements, and indigenous defense procurement.
- **Action:** Bullish on large-cap domestic EPC infrastructure conglomerates, heavy electrical transmission equipment manufacturers, commercial vehicle (HCV) OEMs, and defense electronics suppliers; Bearish on balance-sheet-constrained municipal contractors burdened by elongated working-capital cycles and over-leveraged commercial real estate developers.
