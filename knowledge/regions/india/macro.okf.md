# India Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Calibrated Policy Rate Hikes & Two-Way Interbank Liquidity Steering
- **Condition:** The RBI MPC executes calibrated 25 bps repo rate hikes while utilizing alternating fine-tuning operations (VRR injections and VRRR absorptions) to anchor the Weighted Average Call Rate (WACR) strictly around the policy corridor.
- **Action:** Bullish on 1-year Overnight Index Swaps (OIS) paying positions, ultra-short duration liquidity funds, and high-CASA Tier-1 commercial banks; Bearish on benchmark 10-year G-Secs, corporate bond duration funds, and wholesale-funded non-banking financial companies (NBFCs).

## Rule 2: CPI Acceleration Toward Tolerance Ceiling via Food and Precious Metal Shocks
- **Condition:** Headline retail inflation prints near the RBI's tolerance ceiling toward 5.54% (marking a 23-month high), driven by acute domestic food shocks (e.g., doubling onion prices), energy risks, and elevated precious metals pricing.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic agrochemical producers, fertilizer distributors, upstream resource producers with contractual pass-through pricing, and short-duration sovereign paper; Bearish on volume-sensitive mass-market FMCG manufacturers, entry-level auto OEMs, and price-sensitive consumer durable fabricators.

## Rule 3: EBLR Transmission Disparity & Deposit Repricing Lag
- **Condition:** Successive repo rate hikes transmit immediately to floating-rate retail and MSME loans via External Benchmark Lending Rates (EBLRs), while commercial bank term deposit rate revisions (across SBI, ICICI, HDFC, and Indian Bank) lag asset yields.
- **Action:** Bullish on Tier-1 mega-cap commercial banks with superior low-cost retail deposit franchises, wealth management platforms, and Asset Management Companies (AMCs); Bearish on standalone retail housing finance companies (HFCs), unhedged vehicle financiers, and liability-constrained mid-tier private banks.

## Rule 4: Geopolitical Energy Spillover & Downstream Margin Compression
- **Condition:** West Asia turmoil and geopolitical friction trigger spikes in imported crude oil and maritime shipping freight rates while domestic retail auto fuel prices remain regulated by the government.
- **Action:** Bullish on upstream domestic oil and gas exploration and production (E&P) firms with unhedged crude realization upside, domestic coal extractors, and commercial renewable independent power producers (IPPs); Bearish on state-owned downstream Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs), commercial aviation carriers, and synthetic specialty chemical and paint manufacturers.

## Rule 5: Institutional Liquidity Cleavage & FPI Flow Divergence
- **Condition:** Countervailing market flows emerge as domestic institutional retail SIP inflows absorb episodic foreign portfolio investor (FPI) liquidations and net outflows driven by global risk-off sentiment.
- **Action:** Bullish on Nifty 50 large-cap bellwethers, domestic equity exchange operators, central securities depositories, and high-dividend public sector enterprises; Bearish on richly valued small-and-mid-cap (SMID) equities trading at historic valuation premiums and cash-burning growth tech firms.

## Rule 6: BoP Surplus Expansion & Sovereign FX Band Defense
- **Condition:** Foreign capital dynamics and trade credential validations generate balance sheet resilience, enabling proactive RBI spot and forward interventions to suppress USD/INR implied volatility within a narrow trading corridor.
- **Action:** Bullish on USD/INR carry trades via short offshore forward points, Indian sovereign credit default swap (CDS) tightening, Tier-1 IT services exporters, and contract research/pharma exporters; Bearish on corporates carrying unhedged External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) and import-reliant consumer electronics assemblers.

## Rule 7: Sovereign Trade Credential Upgrades & "China+1" Capex Expansion
- **Condition:** Multilateral validation of upgraded sovereign trade credentials and expanded bilateral industrial corridors accelerate private greenfield manufacturing investment and global supply-chain relocation into India.
- **Action:** Bullish on Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) providers, precision heavy engineering exporters, multi-modal container rail logistics operators, and private deep-water port operators; Bearish on unorganized low-value textile mills, sub-scale bulk commodity chemical producers, and secondary metal fabricators facing regional dumping.
