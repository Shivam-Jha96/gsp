# India Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Calibrated Policy Tightening & Interbank Liquidity Contraction
- **Condition:** The RBI MPC maintains a hawkish bias flagging calibrated rate hikes with rate cuts explicitly ruled out, while systemic banking liquidity contracts abruptly toward structural deficit (surplus dropping below ₹1,000 crore).
- **Action:** Bullish on 1-year Overnight Index Swaps (OIS) paying positions, ultra-short liquidity funds, and high-CASA Tier-1 commercial banks; Bearish on benchmark 10-year G-Secs, corporate bond duration funds, and wholesale-dependent non-banking financial companies (NBFCs).

## Rule 2: Widespread CPI Acceleration Under 2024=100 Base Series
- **Condition:** Headline retail inflation under the revised 2024=100 base series accelerates past 5.50% toward the RBI's 6.00% upper tolerance ceiling, driven by broad price diffusion across food baskets, sticky services, and imported inputs.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic agrochemical producers, fertilizer manufacturers, upstream extractors with contractual cost pass-through capabilities, and short-duration inflation hedges; Bearish on volume-sensitive mass-market FMCG manufacturers, entry-level auto OEMs, and margin-compressed consumer durable fabricators.

## Rule 3: Instantaneous EBLR Transmission & Retail Liability Competition
- **Condition:** Policy rate increases transmit rapidly through External Benchmark Lending Rates (EBLRs) to retail credit, lengthening home loan tenures and compelling scheduled commercial banks to aggressively bid up fixed deposit rates above 8.00% to fund balance sheet growth.
- **Action:** Bullish on Tier-1 mega-cap commercial banks with superior low-cost CASA franchises, wealth management platforms, and Asset Management Companies (AMCs) capturing household financialization; Bearish on standalone retail housing finance companies (HFCs), unhedged vehicle financiers, and liability-constrained mid-tier private lenders.

## Rule 4: Sovereign BoP Surplus & Foreign Exchange Reserve Accretion
- **Condition:** Cumulative net external capital inflows exceeding $50 billion expand sovereign foreign exchange reserves toward the $800 billion mark, generating a robust Balance of Payments (BoP) surplus that shields the Rupee from global foreign exchange shocks.
- **Action:** Bullish on USD/INR carry trades via short offshore forward points, Indian sovereign credit default swap (CDS) compression, Tier-1 IT services exporters, and contract research/pharma exporters; Bearish on corporates carrying high unhedged External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) and import-reliant consumer electronics assemblers.

## Rule 5: Geopolitical Energy Shocks & Downstream Margin Compression
- **Condition:** Escalating Middle Eastern geopolitical tensions drive spikes in imported crude oil prices above baseline assumptions while domestic retail automotive fuel prices remain politically capped or frozen.
- **Action:** Bullish on upstream domestic oil and gas exploration and production (E&P) firms with unhedged realization upside, domestic coal producers, and utility-scale renewable power generators; Bearish on state-owned downstream Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs), commercial aviation carriers, and synthetic specialty chemical/paint manufacturers.

## Rule 6: Dual Institutional Flow Divergence & Large-Cap Valuation Primacy
- **Condition:** Positive Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI) equity inflows coincide with resilient domestic Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) contributions, directing liquidity predominantly into benchmark index heavyweights while higher risk-free rates compress small-and-mid-cap multiples.
- **Action:** Bullish on Nifty 50 large-cap bellwethers, domestic equity exchange operators, custody banks, and dividend-rich cash-generative PSUs; Bearish on richly valued small-and-mid-cap (SMID) equities trading at historical valuation premiums and unprofitable tech platform startups.

## Rule 7: Sovereign Trade Credential Upgrades & "China+1" Capex Expansion
- **Condition:** Multilateral upgrades of sovereign trade credentials alongside expanding bilateral trade integration accelerate private greenfield industrial investment and supply-chain relocations into India.
- **Action:** Bullish on Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) providers, precision heavy engineering exporters, multi-modal container rail freight operators, and private deep-water port operators; Bearish on unorganized low-value textile mills, sub-scale bulk commodity chemical producers, and secondary metal fabricators vulnerable to regional import dumping.
