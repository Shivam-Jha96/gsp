# India Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: RBI Rate-Tightening Cycle Resumption & Yield Curve Bear-Flattening
- **Condition:** The RBI MPC delivers its first rate hike in nearly four years, lifting the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50% amid geopolitical friction and headline inflation crossing above the 4.00% midpoint target, signaling calibrated policy tightening.
- **Action:** Bullish on 1-year and 2-year Overnight Index Swaps (OIS) paying positions, short-duration Treasury Bills (91-day/182-day), and high-CASA Tier-1 commercial banks; Bearish on benchmark 10-year G-Secs, long-duration debt mutual funds, and rate-sensitive wholesale-funded non-banking financial companies (NBFCs).

## Rule 2: CPI Target Breach Under Revised Base & Food Price Pressures
- **Condition:** Retail CPI inflation calculated under the revised 2024=100 base series breaches the RBI's 4.00% target for multiple consecutive months (accelerating past 4.45%), driven by vegetable basket volatility and persistent supply chain bottlenecks.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic agrochemical producers, fertilizer manufacturers, upstream oil & gas exploration entities, and primary commodity producers with contractual cost pass-through capabilities; Bearish on volume-sensitive mass-market FMCG manufacturers, entry-level automotive OEMs, and margin-compressed consumer durable fabricators.

## Rule 3: EBLR Transmission, Mortgage Tenure Strains & Retail Liability Competition
- **Condition:** Policy rate hikes transmit instantaneously via External Benchmark Lending Rates (EBLRs) to retail home loans, forcing borrowing tenure extensions and driving commercial bank fixed deposit rates toward 8.25% to fund credit growth.
- **Action:** Bullish on Tier-1 mega-cap commercial banks with dominant low-cost CASA franchises, wealth management platforms, and Asset Management Companies (AMCs) capturing deposit disintermediation; Bearish on standalone retail housing finance companies (HFCs), vehicle financiers facing margin compression, and wholesale liability-reliant mid-sized lenders.

## Rule 4: Sovereign FX Reserve Expansion Toward $800B & Rupee Resilience
- **Condition:** Massive external capital inflows and surging FCNR deposits push sovereign foreign exchange reserves toward the $800 billion threshold, generating a projected multi-billion-dollar BoP surplus and solidifying Rupee stability against broad USD strength.
- **Action:** Bullish on USD/INR carry trades via short offshore forward points, Indian sovereign credit default swap (CDS) tightening, Tier-1 IT services exporters, and contract research/pharma exporters; Bearish on state-owned Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs) subject to retail fuel price interventions, import-reliant consumer electronics assemblers, and corporates carrying unhedged External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs).

## Rule 5: Dual Institutional Liquidity Convergence & Large-Cap Valuation Primacy
- **Condition:** Above-consensus GDP growth combines with sustained monthly Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI) equity inflows and programmatic domestic Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) contributions, directing institutional capital predominantly into high-liquidity assets.
- **Action:** Bullish on Nifty 50 large-cap bellwethers, domestic equity exchange operators, custody banks/brokerages, and cash-rich dividend-yielding public sector undertakings (PSUs); Bearish on micro-cap and small-and-mid-cap (SMID) equities trading at extreme valuation premiums, speculative thematic baskets, and unprofitable tech platforms.

## Rule 6: Sovereign Trade Credential Upgrades & "China+1" Industrial Capex
- **Condition:** Formal upgrades in sovereign trade credentials, expanding bilateral trade corridors, and global supply-chain diversification drive sustained institutional and foreign direct investment into domestic manufacturing ecosystems.
- **Action:** Bullish on Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) providers, specialized heavy engineering exporters, multi-modal rail freight operators, and private commercial port operators; Bearish on unorganized low-value textile producers, sub-scale commodity chemical producers, and secondary metal fabricators vulnerable to regional import dumping.
