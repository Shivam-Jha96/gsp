# United Kingdom Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Imminent Monetary Tightening and Front-End Rate Repricing
- **Condition:** Upside inflation surprises (e.g., headline CPI climbing to 3.5%) alongside resilient growth prompt the Bank of England MPC to prepare an active Q4 interest rate hike, entirely unwinding easing expectations and pricing in additional policy tightening.
- **Action:** Bearish on short-dated (2Y) UK Gilts and front-quarter SONIA futures; bullish on front-end SONIA implied rate hike pricing and 1Y Forward Rate Agreements (FRAs).

## Rule 2: Inflationary Upside Shocks and Breakeven Widening
- **Condition:** Headline UK CPI jumps above consensus forecasts to 3.5% driven by services persistence and energy pass-through, widening the divergence between inflation expectations and the BoE's 2.0% target.
- **Action:** Bullish on UK 5Y and 10Y inflation-linked Gilts (breakeven inflation rates) and FTSE 100 integrated energy producers; bearish on intermediate nominal Gilts and consumer discretionary equities.

## Rule 3: Autumn Budget Deficit Expansion and Gilt Curve Steepening
- **Condition:** Escalating public sector borrowing data ahead of Chancellor Healey’s Autumn Budget raises fiscal deficit concerns, forcing the UK Debt Management Office (DMO) to expand gilt auction volumes and driving fiscal term premia higher.
- **Action:** Bearish on 10Y and 30Y nominal UK Gilts; bullish on UK Gilt 2s10s and 5s30s yield curve steepeners.

## Rule 4: Monthly GDP Resilience and Rate-Cut Elimination
- **Condition:** Monthly UK GDP prints beat consensus expectations (e.g., expanding 0.1% month-over-month), demonstrating underlying cyclical resilience and reinforcing the MPC's hawkish policy resolve.
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end UK real yields and clearing bank net interest margins; bearish on long-duration sovereign fixed income.

## Rule 5: Sterling Yield Advantage and Divergent FX Appreciation
- **Condition:** Imminent BoE rate hike expectations contrast directly with ongoing easing cycles at the European Central Bank and Federal Reserve, widening UK-EU and UK-US front-end sovereign yield spreads.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, and sterling call options; bearish on EUR/GBP.

## Rule 6: Disappearing Sub-5% Mortgages and Credit Contraction
- **Condition:** Sustained front-end swap rate repricing causes fixed mortgage rates below 5% to vanish across major high-street lenders, compounding debt-servicing friction for refinancing households and tightening domestic credit standards.
- **Action:** Bearish on UK residential homebuilders, domestic mortgage banks, and commercial property REITs; bullish on defensive consumer staples and high-yielding utilities.

## Rule 7: Active Quantitative Tightening and Gilt Swap Spread Cheapening
- **Condition:** The Bank of England sustains active gilt portfolio sales alongside balance sheet runoff during heavy DMO issuance cycles, saturating primary dealer balance sheets with sovereign paper.
- **Action:** Bearish on 10Y and 30Y nominal Gilts; bullish on 10Y and 30Y SONIA swap spreads (Gilts cheapening relative to swaps) and sterling repo rates.

## Rule 8: Multinational Large-Cap Outperformance vs. Domestic Cyclicals
- **Condition:** Domestic stagflationary pressures (3.5% CPI, rising mortgage costs, and tax/borrowing burdens) compress UK household disposable income, while global revenue generation insulates mega-cap exporters.
- **Action:** Bullish on the FTSE 100 / FTSE 250 equity spread (long FTSE 100, short FTSE 250); bearish on domestically focused UK mid-cap retailers and industrial cyclicals.
