# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Sovereign Yield Breakouts, Behind-the-Curve Fears, and Discretionary Rinban Interventions
- **Condition:** Sovereign yields across 10Y–40Y JGBs surge to multi-decade highs amid mounting market perceptions that the BOJ is behind the inflation curve, precipitating disorderly auction tails and forcing the central bank into unscheduled emergency bond purchases (*Rinban*) to slow yield curve steepening.
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end JGB discount bills, domestic money center commercial banks, and tactical curve-flattener unwinds; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (20Y–40Y JGBs), long-duration domestic bond index funds, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Target-Exceeding Core CPI, Hawkish Normalization, and Systematic FX Carry Unwinds
- **Condition:** Japan’s core CPI prints persistently above the 2.0% price stability target—accelerating on weak-yen import pass-through and energy inflation—reinforcing market pricing for consecutive BOJ policy rate hikes and triggering sharp short-covering across global short-JPY carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY) and unhedged export-dependent automotive manufacturers.

## Rule 3: Geopolitical Energy Cost-Push Shocks, Weak Yen Pass-Through, and Utility Margin Compression
- **Condition:** Escalating geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East and crude oil/LNG supply friction compound currency-driven imported cost-push inflation, while the tapering of domestic electricity and city gas subsidies generates consumer price volatility and squeezes downstream margins.
- **Action:** Bullish on diversified Japanese trading houses (*sogo shosha*), domestic inflation-linked bonds (JGBi), and upstream petroleum refiners; Bearish on regulated regional electric and gas utilities constrained by retail tariff lag, and domestic food processors unable to pass through input price hikes.

## Rule 4: Sluggish Household Consumption vs. Resilient Corporate Capex Divergence
- **Condition:** Real GDP prints sub-consensus annualized growth (hovering near 1.1%) due to real wage erosion dampening private consumption, while headline capex is revised upward on robust corporate capital expenditure in semiconductor facilities, software automation, and industrial modernization.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE) makers, factory automation robotics producers, and enterprise software systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market apparel retailers, department store operators, and domestic consumer discretionary cyclicals.

## Rule 5: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Reforms and Balance-Sheet Optimization
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) regulatory mandates targeting companies trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) accelerate the corporate unwinding of strategic cross-shareholdings, the adoption of strict ROE thresholds, and record accretive share repurchases.
- **Action:** Bullish on cash-rich low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms with accelerating net buyback yields, and financial groups divesting legacy corporate equity stakes; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched cross-holdings, and stagnant dividend payout ratios.

## Rule 6: Policy Rate Normalization, Equity Discount Rate Shocks, and Value-over-Growth Rotation
- **Condition:** Formal policy rate hikes and rising domestic risk-free discount rates drive multiple compression across high-duration, high-multiple equities, prompting broad institutional factor rotation toward cash-generative value equities and financial assets with asset-sensitive margins.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, multi-line property and casualty insurers, and regional banking groups; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio, and domestic high-valuation SaaS providers.

## Rule 7: Institutional Asset Repatriation Driven by Inverted Hedged Foreign Yields
- **Condition:** Rising domestic 10Y–30Y JGB yields, coupled with elevated FX-hedging costs on foreign sovereign debt, push net hedged returns on US Treasuries and European sovereign bonds deeply negative, driving Japanese institutional lifers and pension funds to repatriate foreign portfolios into domestic paper.
- **Action:** Bullish on high-grade yen-denominated corporate credit spreads, primary 10Y–20Y domestic sovereign bond auctions, and domestic fixed-income mutual fund inflows; Bearish on FX-hedged US Treasuries, European core sovereign debt, and offshore fixed-income investment trusts (*toshin*).

## Rule 8: Intra-Asian Advanced Intermediate Exports vs. Domestic Fabrication Margin Squeeze
- **Condition:** Sustained East Asian manufacturing momentum and regional technology capex boost demand for Japanese high-spec chemicals, electronic components, and advanced capital inputs, while domestic non-export fabricators suffer from imported raw material price inflation.
- **Action:** Bullish on high-performance electronic materials producers, specialized fine chemical exporters, and intra-Asia container shipping operators; Bearish on low-margin domestic intermediate fabricators and unhedged raw-materials-dependent domestic packaging suppliers.
