# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Sovereign Yield Breakouts, Behind-the-Curve Pressures, and Emergency Rinban Interventions
- **Condition:** Benchmark 10Y–40Y JGB yields spike to multi-decade highs under mounting market perception that the BOJ is behind the inflation curve, triggering auction tails and severe debt-servicing friction that compel the central bank to execute unscheduled emergency bond purchases (*Rinban*).
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end short-duration JGB bills, money center commercial banks benefiting from asset repricing, and tactical curve-flattener positions; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (20Y–40Y JGBs), broad domestic fixed-income benchmarks, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Above-Target Core Inflation, Hawkish Policy Surprises, and Carry Trade Liquidation
- **Condition:** Japan’s core CPI prints persistently above the 2.0% stability target despite administrative energy subsidies, forcing the BOJ into hawkish policy rate hikes that narrow sovereign interest rate differentials and trigger violent short-covering across global short-JPY carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY), emerging market local currency assets funded in yen, and unhedged export-dependent automakers.

## Rule 3: Fed-BOJ Divergence, US Yield Outperformance, and MOF Intervention Triggers
- **Condition:** Upside surprises in US inflation and resilient Federal Reserve terminal rate pricing expand the US-Japan 10Y sovereign yield spread past critical thresholds, driving USD/JPY toward multi-decade highs (155–160 range) and precipitating unilateral Ministry of Finance (MOF) currency market interventions.
- **Action:** Bullish on long USD/JPY momentum prior to intervention levels, export-oriented trading houses (*sogo shosha*), and multinational capital goods exporters; Bearish on import-reliant domestic retail chains, unhedged international air passenger carriers, and domestic food processors.

## Rule 4: Commodity Cost-Push Shocks, Weak Yen Pass-Through, and Utility Margin Squeeze
- **Condition:** Geopolitical disruption and crude oil/LNG price spikes inflate imported fuel costs against a depreciated yen, while regulated retail tariff adjustment caps prevent domestic utilities from passing through generation costs in a timely manner.
- **Action:** Bullish on diversified upstream trading houses, domestic inflation-linked sovereign bonds (JGBi), and petroleum refiners; Bearish on regional electric and gas utilities with rigid retail rate caps, and domestic food manufacturers exposed to imported agricultural commodities.

## Rule 5: Technical Economic Contraction vs. Resilient Modernization and AI Capex
- **Condition:** Headline real GDP unexpectedly contracts annualized (sub-zero to sub-consensus) as eroded household real wages suppress private consumption, while corporate investments in semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE), factory automation, and digital software infrastructure remain structurally insulated.
- **Action:** Bullish on semiconductor manufacturing equipment makers, factory automation robotics, and mission-critical enterprise systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market retail chains, department store operators, and domestic consumer discretionary cyclicals.

## Rule 6: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Mandates and Capital Allocation Optimization
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) regulatory directives targeting persistent price-to-book discounts (PBR < 1.0x) accelerate corporate unwinding of strategic cross-shareholdings, enforce return-on-equity (ROE) hurdles, and drive record accretive share buyback programs.
- **Action:** Bullish on cash-rich low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms expanding net buyback yields, and financial conglomerates divesting cross-held equity portfolios; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched strategic cross-holdings, and passive dividend policies.

## Rule 7: Policy Rate Normalization, Risk-Free Discount Shocks, and Value-over-Growth Rotation
- **Condition:** Successive BOJ policy rate increases elevate domestic risk-free discount rates, causing long-duration equity valuation multiples to compress and driving institutional capital rotation toward asset-sensitive, high-dividend value equities.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, multi-line property and casualty insurers, and regional banking groups; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, domestic software-as-a-service (SaaS) providers, and the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio.

## Rule 8: Inverted FX-Hedged Foreign Yields and Institutional Capital Repatriation
- **Condition:** Rising domestic long-term JGB yields combined with prohibitive foreign-currency hedging costs sustain deeply negative net hedged returns on foreign sovereign debt, incentivizing Japanese life insurers and pension funds to reallocate capital into domestic fixed income.
- **Action:** Bullish on 10Y–20Y primary JGB auctions, yen-denominated investment-grade corporate credit spreads, and domestic institutional fixed-income funds; Bearish on FX-hedged US Treasuries, European core sovereign debt, and offshore fixed-income foreign investment trusts (*toshin*).

## Rule 9: Advanced Intermediate Goods Export Demand vs. Domestic Labor Overhead Constraints
- **Condition:** Resilient East Asian high-tech manufacturing sustains strong external demand for Japanese advanced materials, while domestic-oriented intermediate fabricators suffer severe margin erosion from domestic demographic shrinkage and rising nominal labor overhead.
- **Action:** Bullish on electronic materials producers, specialized fine chemical exporters, and intra-Asia container logistics providers; Bearish on low-margin domestic intermediate metal fabricators and labor-intensive domestic packaging producers.

## Rule 10: Fiscal Dominance Friction and Political Headwinds to Rate Normalization
- **Condition:** Rising sovereign debt-servicing expenditures trigger political resistance from fiscal expansionists and government factions against further BOJ interest rate increases, creating institutional friction that caps policy rate expectations while widening fiscal risk premiums at the long end of the curve.
- **Action:** Bullish on gold in JPY terms, multinational corporations with offshore cash reserves, and 10Y–30Y JGB curve-steepening positions; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (30Y–40Y JGBs), regional banks with large unhedged duration portfolios, and spot JPY against physical store-of-value assets.
