# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: JGB Yield Spikes, Unscheduled Rinban Operations, and Curve Dislocation
- **Condition:** Rapid upward pressure on 10Y–40Y sovereign bond yields drives Japanese Government Bond (JGB) yields to multi-decade highs amid BOJ rate-hike expectations and fiscal debt-service concerns, triggering auction tails and secondary market illiquidity that compel the central bank to deploy unscheduled bond-buying (*Rinban*) operations.
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end short-duration sovereign paper (1Y–2Y JGBs), money center megabanks, and tactical 10Y–30Y JGB curve-flattener swaps upon intervention announcements; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign debt (20Y–40Y JGBs), domestic aggregate bond indices, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Core Inflation Acceleration, Hawkish BOJ Pricing, and Global Carry Unwinds
- **Condition:** Core CPI prints reach multi-year highs and remain persistently above the 2.0% price stability target, solidifying market consensus for near-term BOJ benchmark rate increases and triggering rapid short-covering across global low-yielding JPY carry trades amid surging risk aversion.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (front-month gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY), emerging market local currency assets funded in yen, and export-sensitive automotive OEMs.

## Rule 3: Fed-BOJ Policy Divergence, FX Intervention Triggers, and Dollar-Yen Momentum
- **Condition:** Resilient Federal Reserve policy or lagging global rate-cut cycles maintain a wide US-Japan sovereign yield differential, pushing USD/JPY toward critical intervention thresholds and eliciting acute verbal warnings or direct physical currency intervention from the Ministry of Finance (MOF).
- **Action:** Bullish on USD/JPY spot momentum and short-dated USD/JPY call options prior to official intervention levels, export-oriented general trading companies (*sogo shosha*), and multinational capital goods manufacturers; Bearish on domestic import-dependent food retailers, domestic aviation carriers with unhedged dollar fuel liabilities, and unhedged foreign-currency corporate debt issuers.

## Rule 4: Commodity Cost-Push Import Shocks and Regulated Utility Margin Compression
- **Condition:** Global crude oil and LNG prices surge against an entrenched weak yen, inflating imported wholesale producer prices while retail utility tariff regulation and political pushback prevent the rapid pass-through of fuel costs to domestic end-consumers.
- **Action:** Bullish on upstream integrated trading houses, domestic inflation-linked sovereign bonds (JGBi), and domestic petroleum refiners with positive operational cracking margins; Bearish on regional electric power utilities, city gas distribution networks, and margin-squeezed domestic food and beverage manufacturers.

## Rule 5: Technical Economic Contraction vs. Resilient Capex and Semiconductor Equipment
- **Condition:** Headline real GDP unexpectedly contracts (e.g., annualized declines of -0.1%) due to sluggish real household consumption, while corporate Japan concurrently maintains resilient capital expenditure driven by global artificial intelligence (AI) demand, factory automation, and digital transformation.
- **Action:** Bullish on semiconductor front-end and packaging equipment makers, factory automation robotics leaders, and enterprise digital-transformation systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market department stores, domestic consumer cyclicals, and low-margin retail conglomerates.

## Rule 6: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Reforms and Capital Efficiency Unlocking
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) regulatory directives targeting companies trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) accelerate the structural unwind of strategic cross-shareholdings, mandate ROE expansion above the cost of capital, and prompt record corporate share repurchase announcements.
- **Action:** Bullish on low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms with expanding buyback yields, and financial groups monetizing equity investment portfolios; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched cross-shareholdings, and passive capital allocation policies.

## Rule 7: Policy Rate Normalization, Discount Factor Compression, and Value Outperformance
- **Condition:** BOJ policy rate hikes lift the sovereign risk-free rate structure, driving multi-year highs in domestic discount factors, compressing equity duration multiples, and initiating broad institutional capital reallocation into cash-generative value equities.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, non-life insurance carriers, and regional commercial banking networks; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, domestic software-as-a-service (SaaS) platforms, and the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio.

## Rule 8: Inverted FX-Hedged Foreign Debt Returns and Institutional Capital Repatriation
- **Condition:** Elevated short-term foreign currency hedging costs cause net hedged yields on US Treasuries and European sovereign bonds to remain uncompetitive relative to rising domestic JGB yields, driving Japanese life insurers and pension funds to repatriate capital into domestic fixed income.
- **Action:** Bullish on primary 10Y–20Y JGB auction absorption, yen investment-grade domestic corporate credit spreads, and domestic institutional fixed-income liquidity; Bearish on FX-hedged offshore sovereign debt holdings, foreign-currency public investment trusts (*toshin*), and offshore fixed-income custodial assets.

## Rule 9: High-Tech Intermediate Exports vs. Domestic Labor Overhead Constraints
- **Condition:** Robust international demand for advanced electronic chemicals and precision materials expands external trade revenue, while domestic-oriented intermediate manufacturing suffers acute margin erosion from demographic shrinkage and rising nominal labor overhead.
- **Action:** Bullish on specialized electronic material producers, synthetic resin exporters, and regional maritime logistics operators; Bearish on domestic labor-intensive metal fabricators, localized construction materials producers, and low-margin packaging converters.

## Rule 10: Political Fiscal Resistance, Sovereign Debt-Servicing Risks, and Curve Steepening
- **Condition:** Political pushback from fiscal expansionists and parliamentary factions against central bank rate tightening intensifies alongside proposed supplementary stimulus budgets, generating sovereign debt-servicing friction and widening term premiums across long-dated issuance.
- **Action:** Bullish on gold priced in JPY, multinational balance sheets holding foreign-currency liquidity, and 10Y–30Y JGB curve-steepening swap structures; Bearish on 30Y–40Y sovereign bond auctions, long-duration regional bank balance sheets, and unhedged long-end fixed-income mandates.

## Rule 11: Stagflationary Rate Normalization into Economic Contraction and Multiple De-Rating
- **Condition:** The BOJ signals or executes benchmark interest rate hikes to curb imported currency depreciation and persistent cost-push inflation precisely as quarterly domestic GDP contracts, confronting equity markets with monetary tightening during an economic contraction.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Banks and short-term JPY sovereign bills; Bearish on broad Nikkei 225 equity index futures, domestic real estate development firms with floating-rate debt structures, and consumer financing companies.
