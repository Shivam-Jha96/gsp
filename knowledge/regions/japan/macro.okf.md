# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Sovereign Yield Breakouts, Behind-the-Curve Pressures, and Emergency Rinban Interventions
- **Condition:** Sovereign yields across 10Y–40Y JGBs surge to multi-decade highs amid mounting market consensus that the BOJ is behind the inflation curve, producing failed auction tails and fiscal debt-servicing friction that compel the central bank to execute unscheduled emergency bond purchases (*Rinban*) to temper curve steepening.
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end short-duration JGB bills, domestic money center commercial banks, and tactical curve-flattener positions; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (20Y–40Y JGBs), long-duration domestic bond index funds, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Above-Target Core CPI, Hawkish Normalization, and Systematic JPY Carry Unwinds
- **Condition:** Japan’s core CPI prints persistently above the 2.0% price stability target—accelerating on food and imported energy cost pass-through—reinforcing market pricing for consecutive BOJ policy rate hikes and triggering disorderly short-covering across global short-JPY carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY) and unhedged export-dependent automotive manufacturers.

## Rule 3: Commodity Cost-Push Shocks, Weak Yen Pass-Through, and Utility Margin Compression
- **Condition:** Surging global crude oil and LNG prices compound yen-denominated imported inflation, while the phased expiration or tapering of government utility subsidies creates retail tariff lag that severely compresses power and gas provider margins.
- **Action:** Bullish on diversified Japanese trading houses (*sogo shosha*), domestic inflation-linked bonds (JGBi), and upstream petroleum refiners; Bearish on regulated regional electric and gas utilities constrained by retail tariff revision delays, and domestic food processors unable to pass through raw input price spikes.

## Rule 4: Two-Speed Macro Divergence: Sub-Consensus Private Consumption vs. Resilient Modernization Capex
- **Condition:** Headline real GDP prints sub-consensus annualized growth (~1.1%) as persistent real wage erosion suppresses household consumer expenditure, while aggregate capex is upwardly revised on robust corporate investment in semiconductor manufacturing, automation robotics, and digital infrastructure.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE) makers, industrial robotics producers, and enterprise systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market apparel retailers, department store operators, and domestic consumer discretionary cyclicals.

## Rule 5: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Mandates and Balance-Sheet Optimization
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) regulatory directives targeting companies trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) accelerate the corporate liquidation of strategic cross-shareholdings, enforce return-on-equity (ROE) hurdles, and drive record accretive share buyback announcements.
- **Action:** Bullish on cash-rich low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms expanding net buyback yields, and financial groups divesting strategic corporate equity stakes; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched cross-holdings, and stagnant dividend payout policies.

## Rule 6: Policy Rate Normalization, Equity Discount Rate Shocks, and Value-over-Growth Rotation
- **Condition:** Successive BOJ policy rate increases and elevated domestic risk-free discount rates drive multiple compression across long-duration equities, triggering institutional factor rotation toward asset-sensitive, highly cash-generative value equities.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, multi-line property and casualty insurers, and regional banking groups; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, domestic software-as-a-service (SaaS) providers, and the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio.

## Rule 7: Institutional Asset Repatriation Driven by Inverted FX-Hedged Foreign Yields
- **Condition:** Rising domestic long-term JGB yields coupled with high foreign-currency hedging costs sustain negative net hedged returns on US Treasuries and European sovereign debt, driving Japanese life insurers and public pension funds to repatriate capital into domestic fixed-income instruments.
- **Action:** Bullish on 10Y–20Y primary JGB auctions, yen-denominated investment-grade corporate credit spreads, and domestic institutional fixed-income funds; Bearish on FX-hedged US Treasuries, European core sovereign debt, and offshore fixed-income foreign investment trusts (*toshin*).

## Rule 8: Intra-Asian Advanced Intermediate Exports vs. Domestic Fabrication Margin Squeeze
- **Condition:** Robust East Asian semiconductor and manufacturing cycles sustain elevated export demand for Japan’s high-purity chemicals and advanced electronic components, while domestic-oriented intermediate fabricators absorb severe margin compression from unhedged raw material imports.
- **Action:** Bullish on advanced electronic materials producers, specialized fine chemical exporters, and intra-Asia container shipping operators; Bearish on low-margin domestic intermediate fabricators and unhedged raw-materials-dependent domestic packaging manufacturers.
