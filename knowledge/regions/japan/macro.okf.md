# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: JGB Yield Spikes, Unscheduled Rinban Operations, and Curve Dislocation
- **Condition:** Rapid upward pressure on 10Y–40Y sovereign bond yields drives Japanese Government Bond (JGB) yields to multi-decade highs amid BOJ rate-hike expectations and fiscal funding friction, causing auction tails and market dysfunction that compel the central bank to deploy unscheduled bond-buying (*Rinban*) operations.
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end short-duration sovereign paper (1Y–2Y JGBs), money center megabanks, and tactical 10Y–30Y JGB curve-flattener swaps upon intervention announcements; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign debt (20Y–40Y JGBs), domestic fixed-income aggregate indices, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Core Inflation Persistence, Hawkish BOJ Shifts, and Global Carry Unwinds
- **Condition:** Core and core-core CPI print persistently above the 2.0% price stability target—amplified by rising import fuel costs and service-sector price pass-through—cementing baseline expectations for BOJ policy rate hikes and triggering rapid short-covering across global short-JPY carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (front-month gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY), emerging market local currency assets funded in yen, and export-sensitive automotive original equipment manufacturers (OEMs).

## Rule 3: Fed-BOJ Policy Divergence, US-Japan Yield Differentials, and MOF Intervention Triggers
- **Condition:** US rate cuts are delayed or resilient Federal Reserve policy widens the US-Japan sovereign 10-year yield differential, driving USD/JPY toward the 155–160 intervention threshold and triggering escalating verbal jawboning or direct currency intervention from the Ministry of Finance (MOF).
- **Action:** Bullish on USD/JPY spot momentum and short-dated USD/JPY calls prior to official intervention levels, export-oriented general trading companies (*sogo shosha*), and multinational capital goods manufacturers; Bearish on domestic import-dependent food retailers, domestic aviation carriers with unhedged dollar fuel liabilities, and unhedged foreign-currency corporate debt issuers.

## Rule 4: Commodity Cost-Push Import Shocks and Regulated Utility Margin Compression
- **Condition:** Global crude oil and LNG prices surge against a structurally weak yen, driving imported wholesale inflation higher while retail utility tariff regulation and political resistance prevent the timely pass-through of fuel costs to domestic end-consumers.
- **Action:** Bullish on upstream integrated trading houses, domestic inflation-linked sovereign bonds (JGBi), and domestic petroleum refiners with positive operational cracking margins; Bearish on regional electric power utilities, city gas distribution networks, and margin-squeezed domestic food and beverage manufacturers.

## Rule 5: Technical Economic Contraction vs. Structural Enterprise Modernization Capex
- **Condition:** Headline real GDP registers unexpected annualized contractions or sub-consensus prints due to weak domestic household consumption, while corporate Japan simultaneously posts resilient, structural capital expenditure in semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE), factory automation, and digital enterprise software.
- **Action:** Bullish on semiconductor front-end and packaging equipment makers, factory automation robotics leaders, and enterprise digital-transformation systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market department stores, domestic consumer cyclicals, and low-margin retail conglomerates.

## Rule 6: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Reforms and Capital Efficiency Unlocking
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) regulatory directives penalizing companies trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) accelerate the structural unwind of strategic cross-shareholdings, mandate ROE expansion above the cost of capital, and drive record corporate share repurchase authorizations.
- **Action:** Bullish on low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms with expanding buyback yields, and financial groups monetizing equity portfolios; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched strategic cross-shareholdings, and unresponsive capital allocation policies.

## Rule 7: Policy Rate Normalization, Discount Factor Compression, and Value Equity Outperformance
- **Condition:** BOJ policy rate increases elevate the domestic risk-free rate structure, driving multi-year highs in domestic discount factors, compressing equity duration multiples, and initiating broad institutional capital reallocation into cash-generative value equities.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, non-life insurance carriers, and regional commercial banking networks; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, domestic software-as-a-service (SaaS) platforms, and the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio.

## Rule 8: Inverted FX-Hedged Foreign Debt Returns and Institutional Capital Repatriation
- **Condition:** Elevated short-term foreign currency hedging costs cause net hedged yields on US Treasuries and European sovereign bonds to remain negative or unattractive relative to rising domestic yields, driving life insurers and domestic pension funds to repatriate capital into domestic fixed income.
- **Action:** Bullish on primary 10Y–20Y JGB auction absorption, yen investment-grade domestic corporate credit spreads, and domestic institutional fixed-income liquidity; Bearish on FX-hedged offshore sovereign debt holdings, foreign-currency public investment trusts (*toshin*), and offshore fixed-income custodial assets.

## Rule 9: High-Tech Intermediate Materials Exports vs. Domestic Labor Overhead Constraints
- **Condition:** Robust international and intra-Asian demand for advanced electronic chemicals and precision materials expands external trade revenue, while domestic-oriented intermediate manufacturing suffers acute margin erosion from demographic shrinkage and rising nominal labor overhead.
- **Action:** Bullish on specialized electronic material producers, synthetic resin exporters, and regional maritime logistics operators; Bearish on domestic labor-intensive metal fabricators, localized construction materials producers, and low-margin packaging converters.

## Rule 10: Fiscal Dominance Politics and Term-Premium Sovereign Curve Steepening
- **Condition:** Political resistance from fiscal expansionists and parliamentary factions against central bank rate tightening intensifies alongside proposed supplementary stimulus budgets, generating sovereign debt-servicing friction and widening term premiums across long-dated issuance.
- **Action:** Bullish on gold priced in JPY, multinational balance sheets holding foreign-currency liquidity, and 10Y–30Y JGB curve-steepening swap structures; Bearish on 30Y–40Y sovereign bond auctions, long-duration regional bank balance sheets, and unhedged long-end fixed-income mandates.

## Rule 11: Stagflationary Rate Normalization into GDP Contraction and Equity Multiple De-Rating
- **Condition:** The BOJ signals or executes benchmark rate hikes to curb currency weakness and persistent cost-push inflation precisely as quarterly domestic GDP contracts or misses consensus, confronting the market with monetary tightening into an economic downturn.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Banks and short-term JPY sovereign bills; Bearish on broad Nikkei 225 equity index futures, domestic real estate development firms with floating-rate debt structures, and consumer financing companies.
