# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Sovereign Yield Breakouts, Behind-the-Curve Fears, and Discretionary Rinban Interventions
- **Condition:** Sovereign yields across 10Y–40Y JGBs surge to multi-decade highs amid accelerating bets on BOJ rate hikes, triggering market debate over whether the central bank is behind the curve and forcing speculation of unscheduled emergency bond-buying (*Rinban*) operations to prevent disorderly auction tails and rapid yield curve steepening.
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end JGB discount bills, domestic money center commercial banks, and tactical curve-flattener unwinds; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (20Y–40Y JGBs), long-duration domestic bond index funds, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Target-Exceeding Core CPI, Hawkish Normalization, and Systematic FX Carry Unwinds
- **Condition:** Japan’s core CPI prints top the 2.0% price stability target (reaching 2.1%+ driven by import costs and energy pass-through) while the BOJ prepares for further policy rate hikes, triggering aggressive short-covering in the Japanese Yen and driving broad-based liquidations of global short-JPY carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY) and unhedged export-dependent automotive manufacturers.

## Rule 3: Resurgent Energy Cost-Push Shocks vs. Government Utility Subsidy Distortion
- **Condition:** Rebounding crude oil and LNG prices reignite imported cost-push inflation and lift core goods prices, while the phase-out or re-imposition of electricity and city gas subsidies generates headline CPI volatility and compresses corporate operating margins.
- **Action:** Bullish on diversified Japanese trading conglomerates (*sogo shosha*), domestic inflation-linked bonds (JGBi), and upstream petroleum refiners; Bearish on regulated regional electric and gas utilities constrained by regulatory price lags, and domestic food processors unable to execute unhedged cost pass-through.

## Rule 4: Sluggish Household Consumption vs. Upwardly Revised Corporate Capex Divergence
- **Condition:** Real GDP prints sluggish annualized growth (around 1.1%, missing top-line expectations) due to real wage drags on private household consumption, while headline revisions reflect resilient domestic enterprise capex in semiconductor manufacturing capacity, digitization, and automation.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE) producers, factory automation robotics makers, and enterprise software systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market apparel retailers, department store operators, and domestic consumer discretionary cyclicals.

## Rule 5: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Reforms and Balance-Sheet Optimization
- **Condition:** Persistent Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) compliance pressure targeting companies trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) accelerates corporate unwinding of cross-shareholdings, adoption of binding Return on Equity (ROE) targets, and execution of record accretive share repurchases.
- **Action:** Bullish on cash-rich low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms with accelerating net buyback yields, and financial groups divesting legacy corporate cross-holdings; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched cross-shareholding structures, and stagnant payout ratios.

## Rule 6: Policy Rate Normalization, Equity Discount Rate Shocks, and Value-over-Growth Rotation
- **Condition:** Hawkish BOJ policy communication and escalating terminal rate expectations push up the domestic risk-free discount rate, prompting multiple compression across high-duration tech equities and triggering massive factor rotation toward cash-generative value equities.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, multi-line property and casualty insurers, and regional banking groups; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio, and domestic high-valuation SaaS providers.

## Rule 7: Institutional Asset Repatriation Driven by Inverted Hedged Foreign Yields
- **Condition:** Rising domestic 10Y–30Y JGB yields, coupled with elevated FX-hedging costs on US Dollar and Euro assets, keep net hedged foreign sovereign yields deeply uncompetitive, forcing Japanese institutional lifers and pension funds to reallocate offshore capital into domestic credit.
- **Action:** Bullish on high-grade yen-denominated corporate credit spreads, primary 10Y–20Y domestic sovereign bond auctions, and domestic fixed-income mutual fund inflows; Bearish on FX-hedged US Treasuries, European core sovereign debt, and offshore fixed-income investment trusts (*toshin*).

## Rule 8: Intra-Asian Intermediate Export Momentum vs. Domestic Fabrication Margin Squeeze
- **Condition:** Resilient East Asian trade activity and above-consensus regional manufacturing demand support export volumes of Japanese high-spec chemicals, specialized materials, and industrial components, while purely domestic fabricators face severe margin compression from imported feedstock costs.
- **Action:** Bullish on high-performance electronic materials producers, specialized fine chemical exporters, and intra-Asia container shipping operators; Bearish on domestic low-margin intermediate fabricators and unhedged raw-materials-dependent domestic packaging suppliers.
