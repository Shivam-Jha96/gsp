# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Ultra-Long Sovereign Debt Friction, Political Fiscal Pushback, and Rinban Interventions
- **Condition:** Bank of Japan policy normalization and quantitative tightening push yields on 20Y–40Y sovereign debt to multi-decade highs, exacerbating government debt-servicing constraints and triggering market expectations of discretionary BOJ emergency bond purchases (Rinban operations) to curb disorderly steepening.
- **Action:** Bullish on 10Y-30Y JGB curve steepeners, front-end JGB discount bills, and domestic money center banks; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (30Y–40Y JGBs) and rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: FX Carry Unwind Asymmetry, Rate Repricing, and Currency Vigilance
- **Condition:** Domestic core CPI prints exceeding the BOJ target accelerate rate hike pricing, narrowing sovereign yield spreads against major global central banks and triggering violent short-covering rallies in the Japanese Yen alongside systematic unwinding of cross-yen carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on tactical spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY) and unhedged export-dependent automotive manufacturers.

## Rule 3: Geopolitical Energy Cost-Push Pressures vs. Government Utility Subsidy Distortions
- **Condition:** Rebounding international crude oil and LNG prices pass through into rising domestic import costs and headline inflation, while underlying consumer price indices fluctuate due to the phase-in, expiration, or reinstatement of government utility subsidies.
- **Action:** Bullish on Japanese general trading houses (*sogo shosha*), domestic inflation-linked bonds (JGBi), and upstream energy refiners; Bearish on regulated electric utilities facing retail margin compression and domestic food processors unable to pass through feedstock costs.

## Rule 4: Structural Capex and AI Infrastructure Resilience vs. Household Consumption Stagnation
- **Condition:** Headline real GDP growth decelerates or misses consensus due to persistent stagnation in real inflation-adjusted household wages and private consumption, while corporate capital expenditure maintains upward momentum driven by semiconductor fab buildouts, factory automation, and enterprise AI adoption.
- **Action:** Bullish on semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME) producers, industrial robotics makers, and enterprise systems integration firms; Bearish on domestic mass-market retailers, passenger rail transit operators, and consumer discretionary cyclicals.

## Rule 5: TSE Balance-Sheet Mandates, Corporate Governance Reforms, and Buyback Acceleration
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange pressure on listed entities trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) compels corporate management to eliminate cross-shareholdings, optimize capital allocation, and deploy retained corporate earnings into record share repurchases and dividend increases.
- **Action:** Bullish on cash-rich low-PBR value equities, capital goods firms with high net buyback yields, and financial institutions unwinding corporate equity cross-holdings; Bearish on governance laggards with sub-5% ROE and defensive poison-pill structures.

## Rule 6: Hawkish Terminal Rate Re-anchoring, Discount Rate Shocks, and Value-over-Growth Rotation
- **Condition:** Persistent core-core inflation prints and hawkish central bank forward guidance lift expectations for the BOJ terminal policy rate, inducing equity discount rate shocks and multiple compression across long-duration growth equities in favor of asset-sensitive value sectors.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, multi-line property and casualty insurers, and regional banking institutions; Bearish on high-P/E software equities, growth-heavy Nikkei 225 constituents, and the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio.

## Rule 7: Institutional Asset Repatriation and Foreign Sovereign Debt Disinvestment
- **Condition:** Elevated yields across domestic 10Y–30Y JGBs, combined with prohibitive currency-hedging costs on US Dollar and Euro assets, invert the FX-hedged yield advantage of foreign sovereign debt, compelling domestic life insurers and pension funds to repatriate portfolio allocations back into domestic fixed income.
- **Action:** Bullish on domestic high-grade yen-denominated corporate credit, primary domestic corporate bond spreads, and 10Y–20Y domestic debt absorption; Bearish on FX-hedged US Treasuries, European core sovereign debt, and offshore fixed-income investment trusts (*toshin*).

## Rule 8: Regional Asian Intermediate Export Impulses vs. Domestic Input Cost Pressures
- **Condition:** Resilient export-led demand across East Asia stimulates localized industrial orders for Japanese high-spec components and advanced production equipment, while domestic-oriented intermediate fabricators suffer margin erosion under elevated import costs.
- **Action:** Bullish on advanced electronic materials suppliers, specialized fine chemical exporters, and regional container shipping lines; Bearish on low-margin domestic intermediate goods producers with high unhedged commodity import exposure.
