# Japan Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Sovereign Yield Breakouts, Behind-the-Curve Pressures, and Emergency Rinban Interventions
- **Condition:** Sovereign yields across 10Y–40Y JGBs break to multi-decade highs amid market consensus that the BOJ is behind the inflation curve or abandoning Yield Curve Control (YCC), generating auction tails and debt-servicing friction that compel the BOJ to deploy unscheduled emergency bond purchases (*Rinban*).
- **Action:** Bullish on front-end short-duration JGB bills, domestic money center commercial banks, and tactical curve-flattener positions; Bearish on ultra-long sovereign paper (20Y–40Y JGBs), broad domestic bond index funds, and interest-rate-sensitive J-REITs.

## Rule 2: Above-Target Core Inflation, Rate-Hike Surprises, and Disorderly Carry Unwinds
- **Condition:** Japan’s core CPI prints persistently above the 2.0% stability target driven by energy and raw material pass-through, forcing the BOJ into hawkish policy rate adjustments that compress sovereign interest rate differentials and trigger violent short-covering across global short-JPY carry trades.
- **Action:** Bullish on spot JPY, long JPY implied volatility (gamma), and short USD/JPY risk reversals; Bearish on high-beta cross-yen carry pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, MXN/JPY), emerging market local currency debt funded in yen, and unhedged export-dependent automakers.

## Rule 3: Fed-BOJ Policy Divergence, US CPI Outperformance, and JPY Depreciation Pressures
- **Condition:** Upside surprises in US inflation and resilient Federal Reserve terminal rate pricing widen the US-Japan 10Y sovereign yield spread, suppressing JPY spot toward multi-decade lows and raising the threshold for unilateral Ministry of Finance (MOF) currency intervention.
- **Action:** Bullish on long USD/JPY, major global Japanese industrial conglomerates with heavy dollar-denominated revenue receipts, and international trading firms; Bearish on import-reliant domestic retailers, unhedged international air passenger carriers, and unhedged domestic food staples.

## Rule 4: Commodity Cost-Push Shocks, Weak Yen Pass-Through, and Utility Margin Compression
- **Condition:** Surging global crude oil and LNG benchmarks accelerate domestic imported energy cost inflation, while regulated fuel-cost pass-through caps and retail tariff adjustment lags prevent domestic power and gas providers from protecting operating margins.
- **Action:** Bullish on diversified Japanese trading houses (*sogo shosha*), domestic inflation-linked bonds (JGBi), and upstream petroleum refiners; Bearish on regional electric and gas utilities with rigid retail rate structures, and domestic food processors facing margin compression on imported agricultural inputs.

## Rule 5: Two-Speed Macro Divergence: Sub-Consensus Private Consumption vs. Resilient Modernization Capex
- **Condition:** Headline real GDP prints sub-consensus annualized growth (~1.1%) as persistent real wage erosion suppresses household discretionary expenditure, while corporate modernization, software investment, and factory automation capex remain resilient.
- **Action:** Bullish on semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE) makers, industrial robotics producers, and enterprise software systems integrators; Bearish on mass-market retail chains, department store operators, and domestic consumer discretionary cyclicals.

## Rule 6: Tokyo Stock Exchange Governance Mandates and Balance-Sheet Optimization
- **Condition:** Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) regulatory directives targeting companies trading below book value (PBR < 1.0x) accelerate corporate unwinding of strategic cross-shareholdings, enforce return-on-equity (ROE) hurdles, and drive record accretive share buyback announcements.
- **Action:** Bullish on cash-rich low-PBR heavy industrials, capital goods firms expanding net buyback yields, and financial groups divesting strategic corporate equity stakes; Bearish on governance laggards with persistent sub-5% ROE, entrenched cross-holdings, and stagnant dividend payout policies.

## Rule 7: Policy Rate Normalization, Equity Discount Rate Shocks, and Value-over-Growth Rotation
- **Condition:** Successive BOJ policy rate increases and elevated domestic risk-free discount rates drive multiple compression across long-duration equities, triggering institutional factor rotation toward asset-sensitive, highly cash-generative value equities.
- **Action:** Bullish on TOPIX Value relative to TOPIX Growth, multi-line property and casualty insurers, and regional banking groups; Bearish on high-multiple growth equities, domestic software-as-a-service (SaaS) providers, and the Nikkei/TOPIX (NT) ratio.

## Rule 8: Institutional Asset Repatriation Driven by Inverted FX-Hedged Foreign Yields
- **Condition:** Rising domestic long-term JGB yields coupled with high foreign-currency hedging costs sustain negative net hedged returns on US Treasuries and European sovereign debt, driving Japanese life insurers and public pension funds to repatriate capital into domestic fixed-income instruments.
- **Action:** Bullish on 10Y–20Y primary JGB auctions, yen-denominated investment-grade corporate credit spreads, and domestic institutional fixed-income funds; Bearish on FX-hedged US Treasuries, European core sovereign debt, and offshore fixed-income foreign investment trusts (*toshin*).

## Rule 9: Advanced Intermediate Export Demand vs. Domestic Labor and Immigration Overhead Squeeze
- **Condition:** Robust Southeast Asian manufacturing and global semiconductor production drive external demand for Japanese advanced materials, while domestic-oriented intermediate fabricators absorb severe margin erosion from acute domestic labor shortages and surging immigration compliance and labor overhead costs.
- **Action:** Bullish on advanced electronic materials producers, specialized fine chemical exporters, and intra-Asia freight forwarders; Bearish on low-margin domestic intermediate metal fabricators and labor-intensive domestic packaging manufacturers.

## Rule 10: Fiscal Dominance Friction and Political Headwinds to Rate Normalization
- **Condition:** Surging sovereign debt-servicing costs trigger political resistance from fiscal expansionists and government leadership against rapid BOJ rate hikes, creating institutional friction that caps policy rate expectations and unanchors long-term fiscal risk premiums.
- **Action:** Bullish on gold in JPY terms, multinational corporations with dominant overseas earnings, and 10Y–30Y JGB curve-steepening positions; Bearish on unhedged domestic bank equities vulnerable to policy rate pauses, long-dated sovereign debt, and spot JPY against physical store-of-value assets.
