# United Kingdom Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Conditional Hawkish Hold and Front-End Rate Repricing
- **Condition:** The Bank of England maintains the Bank Rate at 3.75% while explicitly conditioning late-cycle Q4 rate hikes on sustained Middle East conflict and elevated energy import prices.
- **Action:** Bearish on 2Y UK Gilts and short-dated SONIA futures; bullish on SONIA front-month implied volatility and 1Y forward rate agreements (FRAs).

## Rule 2: Disinflationary Print Relief and Tactical Dovish Retracement
- **Condition:** Monthly headline CPI prints show transient deceleration toward 3.1%, weakening MPC consensus for an immediate Q4 rate hike despite sticky imported energy components.
- **Action:** Bullish on short-dated UK Gilts and SONIA futures contracts; bearish on GBP short-term yield spreads against EUR and USD.

## Rule 3: Energy Pass-Through and Gilt Breakeven Widening
- **Condition:** Geopolitical disruption in the Middle East sustains an energy supply shock, driving headline UK inflation expectations higher while real household consumption remains constrained.
- **Action:** Bullish on UK 5Y and 10Y inflation-linked Gilts (breakevens) and FTSE 100 integrated energy producers; bearish on FTSE 250 Consumer Discretionary equities.

## Rule 4: Sovereign Debt Supply and Maturity Shortening Premia
- **Condition:** Persistent public borrowing warnings from the OBR and HM Treasury coincide with the UK Debt Management Office (DMO) shortening the average maturity of Gilt issuance to absorb fiscal deficits.
- **Action:** Bearish on 10Y and 30Y nominal Gilts; bullish on UK Gilt yield curve steepeners (2s10s and 5s30s) and long-end asset swap spreads.

## Rule 5: Growth Resilience and Monetary Easing Discounting Delay
- **Condition:** Monthly or quarterly UK GDP prints deliver upside expansions (e.g., surprise monthly bounce), demonstrating cyclical resilience that delays financial market pricing of early rate cuts.
- **Action:** Bullish on UK front-end real yields and GBP/EUR; bearish on duration-sensitive UK sovereign bonds.

## Rule 6: Mortgage Refinancing Cliff and Household Credit Impairment
- **Condition:** The transmission of the 3.75% Bank Rate triggers a concentrated wave of household mortgage refinancing into higher fixed borrowing rates, elevating debt-servicing ratios and contracting disposable income.
- **Action:** Bearish on UK residential homebuilders, domestic retail lenders, and commercial REITs; bullish on defensive UK regulated utilities and non-discretionary consumer staples.

## Rule 7: Cabinet Resignations and Political Risk Discounting
- **Condition:** High imported inflation coincides with high-profile domestic political friction and senior cabinet resignations (such as health or treasury ministers), raising fiscal uncertainty while gilts and currency absorb political headlines.
- **Action:** Bearish on GBP/USD and domestic UK corporate credit spreads; bullish on USD-earning multinational FTSE 100 exporters.

## Rule 8: Resource-Exporting Multinationals vs. Domestic Mid-Cap Divergence
- **Condition:** Global commodity benchmarks remain supported by geopolitical risk premia while domestic UK economic activity contends with elevated debt-servicing burdens and tight credit conditions.
- **Action:** Bullish on the FTSE 100 / FTSE 250 equity spread (long FTSE 100, short FTSE 250); bearish on UK small-cap domestic industrials.
