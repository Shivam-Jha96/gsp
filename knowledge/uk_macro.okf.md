# United Kingdom Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Divergent Monetary Policy vs. Federal Reserve
- **Condition:** The Bank of England pauses or keeps rates unchanged while navigating sticky domestic inflation (CPI at 3.1%) and domestic fiscal uncertainty, decoupling from aggressive Fed tightening cycles.
- **Action:** Bearish on GBP relative to USD; bullish on short-to-medium duration UK Gilts as terminal rate expectations are priced lower than US counterparts.

## Rule 2: Energy Shock and Headline Inflation Pressures
- **Condition:** Oil price shocks and high energy costs drive headline CPI inflation above the BoE target (e.g., printing at 3.1%), squeezing household real incomes despite soft broader economic growth.
- **Action:** Bearish on UK Consumer Discretionary equities (FTSE 250 consumer-facing retail); bullish on UK Energy majors (FTSE 100 oil and gas constituents) as a direct inflation hedge.

## Rule 3: Stagnant Growth and Fiscal Expansion Pressures
- **Condition:** Monthly GDP prints show zero or negative growth (e.g., January stagnation) compounding with market anxiety over higher public spending and fiscal expansion under a new Prime Minister.
- **Action:** Bearish on long-dated Gilts due to rising term premia and increased debt issuance risk; neutral to defensive on broader UK domestic equities (FTSE 250).

## Rule 4: Split Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) Dynamics
- **Condition:** MPC members signal internal division—such as dovish members (e.g., Taylor) viewing rate hikes as "not compelling" balanced against hawks (e.g., Ramsden) warning of persistent inflation pressures.
- **Action:** Expect heightened short-term volatility in short Sterling futures and SONIA swap curves around inflation and labor market data releases; maintain tactical range-bound trading strategies on the front end.

## Rule 5: Financial Conditions and Corporate Resilience
- **Condition:** Overall financial conditions remain relatively loose, providing a tailwind to corporate liquidity despite broader economic headwinds and structural energy shocks.
- **Action:** Bullish on UK Financials and export-heavy FTSE 100 multinationals that benefit from global revenue streams while insulating themselves from domestic GDP stagnation.
