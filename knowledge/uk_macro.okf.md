# United Kingdom Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: Conditional Hawkish Hold and Front-End Rate Repricing
- **Condition:** The Bank of England maintains the Bank Rate at 3.75% while explicitly conditioning late-cycle Q4 rate hikes on sustained Middle East conflict and elevated energy import prices.
- **Action:** Bearish on 2Y UK Gilts and short-dated SONIA futures; bullish on SONIA front-month implied volatility and 1Y forward rate agreements (FRAs).

## Rule 2: Disinflationary Print Relief and Tactical Dovish Retracement
- **Condition:** Monthly headline CPI prints show transient deceleration toward 3.1%, weakening the MPC consensus for an immediate Q4 rate hike despite sticky energy inputs.
- **Action:** Bullish on short-dated UK Gilts and SONIA futures contracts; bearish on GBP short-term yield spreads against EUR and USD.

## Rule 3: Energy Pass-Through and Gilt Breakeven Widening
- **Condition:** Geopolitical disruption in the Middle East sustains an energy supply shock, driving headline UK inflation above target while domestic demand fundamentals remain subdued.
- **Action:** Bullish on UK 5Y and 10Y inflation-linked Gilts (breakevens) and FTSE 100 integrated energy producers; bearish on FTSE 250 Consumer Discretionary equities.

## Rule 4: Sovereign Debt Supply and Debt Maturity Shortening Premia
- **Condition:** Persistent public borrowing warnings from the OBR and HM Treasury coincide with the DMO shortening the average maturity of Gilt issuance to absorb fiscal deficits.
- **Action:** Bearish on 10Y and 30Y nominal Gilts; bullish on UK Gilt yield curve steepeners (2s10s and 5s30s) and long-end asset swap spreads.

## Rule 5: Growth Resilience and Monetary Easing Discounting Delay
- **Condition:** Quarterly UK GDP prints surprise to the upside (e.g., 0.6% expansion), demonstrating cyclical resilience that invalidates market pricing of premature policy rate cuts.
- **Action:** Bullish on UK front-end real yields and GBP/EUR; bearish on duration-sensitive UK sovereign bonds.

## Rule 6: Mortgage Refinancing Cliff and Household Credit Impairment
- **Condition:** The transmission of the 3.75% Bank Rate triggers a wave of household mortgage refinancing into significantly higher fixed borrowing costs, elevating debt-servicing ratios and suppressing retail spending.
- **Action:** Bearish on UK residential homebuilders, domestic retail lenders, and commercial REITs; bullish on defensive UK regulated utilities and non-discretionary consumer staples.

## Rule 7: External Terms-of-Trade Deterioration and Political Friction
- **Condition:** High imported energy costs combine with domestic political volatility and cabinet departures (such as senior ministerial resignations), decoupling nominal Gilt yields from Sterling appreciation.
- **Action:** Bearish on GBP/USD and domestic financial credit; bullish on USD-earning multinational FTSE 100 equities.

## Rule 8: Resource-Exporting Multinationals vs. Domestic Mid-Cap Divergence
- **Condition:** Commodity benchmarks remain elevated amidst geopolitical friction while domestic UK economic growth faces structural consumption headwinds and tight credit conditions.
- **Action:** Bullish on the FTSE 100 / FTSE 250 equity spread (long FTSE 100, short FTSE 250); bearish on UK small-cap domestic industrials.
