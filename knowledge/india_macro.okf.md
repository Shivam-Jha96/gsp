# India Macro Trading Rules

## Rule 1: RBI Repo Rate & Growth-Inflation Trade-off
- **Condition:** RBI hikes repo rate or signals tightening due to broadening consumer inflation (e.g., food/fuel spikes, sugar/chip price pressures) while growth remains robust.
- **Action:** Bearish on NIFTY 50 equities, Bullish on INR and Banking sector net interest margins (NIMs) via enhanced asset yields.

## Rule 2: Monsoon Output & Rural Consumption
- **Condition:** Below average monsoon rainfall triggering agricultural output stress and food inflation spikes.
- **Action:** Bearish on FMCG and Auto sectors due to depressed rural disposable incomes, Bullish on structural inflation expectations.

## Rule 3: FPI Flows and FX Dynamics
- **Condition:** Sustained Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) outflows driven by global trade tensions or US-India yield differentials, leading to sharp depreciation pressures on the rupee (INR breaching historical lows).
- **Action:** Bearish on Indian Equities (Broad NIFTY/Sensex), Bearish on INR, while selectively Bullish on export-heavy sectors (IT/Pharma).

## Rule 4: Geopolitical Shocks & Commodity Import Pressures
- **Condition:** Escalating geopolitical conflicts (e.g., Middle East crises) driving up imported energy and raw material costs.
- **Action:** Bearish on India's current account balance and domestic margin-sensitive manufacturing; Bullish on domestic energy alternatives and defensive safe havens.

## Rule 5: Central Bank FX Intervention Divergence
- **Condition:** RBI prioritizes domestic CPI inflation containment over aggressive interest rate defense of the rupee, relying instead on structural capital controls or FCNR(B) inflow incentives.
- **Action:** Neutral-to-Bullish on banking sector liquidity inflows, volatile-to-Bearish on spot INR depending on aggressive dollar-selling episodes by the central bank.
